I recently finished reading *The Formula: The Universal Laws of Success* by Albert-László Barabási. I'm thrilled to recommend this book to anyone looking to understand the science behind human success. Let me share some thoughts about the book.

---

## About The Author

[Albert-László Barabási](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert-L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Barab%C3%A1si), originally from Romania, is a professor at Northeastern University in Boston and a renowned network scientist, best known for his groundbreaking work in the field of complex networks. Barabási introduced the concept of scale-free networks, which describe the highly connected nodes found in many real-world systems. The principles underlying these networks can be applied to various human phenomena, such as the spread of ideas, economic markets, and even - Bingo! - personal success.

---

## What Is Success?

Barabási initially defines success as an objective, measurable outcome that can be quantified through specific metrics, such as the number of citations a scientific paper receives, the total number of followers or likes on a social media account, or the total revenue generated by a business. He also argues that success is not just an individual achievement but a collective phenomenon; it is fundamentally about how others perceive and recognize our achievements.

---

## The Five Universal Laws of Success

The book outlines five universal laws that govern human success:
1. **Performance drives success, but when performance can't be measured, networks drive success.** This law states that in disciplines where performance can be precisely quantified, the most successful individuals will be those with the highest performance - such as the fastest runner, the most viewed YouTuber, or the fastest motorcycle racer. However, in situations where performance is difficult to quantify, like in art or when comparisons are subjective, an individual's success is heavily influenced by their networks and connections.
2. **Performance is bounded, but success is unbounded.** This law highlights the idea that there are natural limits to individual performance - whether in terms of skill, speed, or talent. However, the recognition or success that results from that performance is not constrained in the same way. For example, in sports like tennis, the difference in performance between top-ranked players and those just below them can be minimal, yet their levels of success can vary dramatically.
3. **Previous success \* fitness = future success.** This law suggests that future success is a product of past achievements and ongoing ability or fitness to perform. Also known as preferential attachment, it states that success breeds success: those who have already achieved recognition or success are more likely to attract further opportunities and recognition, thus increasing their chances of future success.
4. **While team success requires diversity and balance, a single individual will receive credit for the group's achievements.** This law explains that success, especially in teams, requires a diversity of perspectives, skills, and experiences that enhance creativity. However, the recognition and rewards associated with the team's success are often disproportionately attributed to a single person. For this reason, it's crucial to build preferential attachment - meaning that individuals should actively work to establish and maintain a strong personal reputation. Balancing contributions to the team with cultivating your own success is essential for long-term career growth.
5. **With persistence success can come at any time.** This law proposes that success has no age and emphasizes the critical role of persistence in achieving success. While performance and networks are important, sustained effort over time is what ultimately leads to lasting success. In essence, the fifth law reminds us that success is not just about being talented or lucky; it's about staying the course, learning from setbacks, and relentlessly pursuing one's goals.

## Final Thoughts

Overall, the book is excellent. I appreciate how it covers a wide range of topics, including science, art, history, and contemporary culture. I also like that the book is accessible to everyone - it's easy to read, and all of the laws are clearly explained through real-world examples.
