# Stablecoins and Tokenized Deposits

Eurodollars carry the dollar outside the United States but keep it inside banks (see [Eurodollars and Central Bank Swap Lines](17-eurodollars-and-central-bank-swap-lines.md)). Stablecoins, the latest large monetary development of the 21st century, carry it outside both.

## What a Stablecoin Is

A stablecoin is a token on a blockchain whose value is anchored at $1, because its issuer promises to pay $1 for it whenever its holder asks. It is therefore a liability at par and on demand, exactly like a bank deposit. These two features, issued on demand and traded at par, make a liability a money claim (see [Cash Pools and Shadow Money](15-cash-pools-and-shadow-money.md)).

On 26 November 2026, stablecoins in circulation were worth $284 billion. The two largest were USDT of Tether, with $174 billion, and USDC of Circle, with $69 billion. The total peaked at $314 billion on 17 May 2026, and on 30 September 2026 it was $306 billion, with $184 billion of USDT and $75 billion of USDC.

## The GENIUS Act

In the United States, the GENIUS Act, signed on 18 July 2025, requires an issuer to back each stablecoin at least one to one with identifiable reserves of these assets.

- US coins and currency, and balances at a Federal Reserve Bank.
- Demand deposits at insured banks.
- Treasury bills, notes and bonds with a remaining maturity of 93 days or less.
- Cash received in overnight repos backed by Treasury bills with a maturity of 93 days or less.
- Overnight reverse repos backed by Treasuries.
- Government money market funds that invest only in the assets above.
- Other similarly liquid assets issued by the federal government, if the regulator approves them.
- Tokenized forms of these assets, except repos.

The issuer cannot pledge the reserves again, and it cannot pay the holder any interest or yield only for holding the stablecoin. If the issuer fails, the holders have a claim on the reserves before all other creditors.

The law takes effect on the earlier of two dates, 18 months after it was signed, which is 18 January 2027, or 120 days after the final regulations. The regulators had to adopt the rules by 18 July 2026 but were late. By late November 2026 only the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency had a final rule for issuers, effective on 18 January 2027, while the Treasury, the FDIC and the Fed had only proposals or interim rules.

## Public Shadow Money

Under this law, a stablecoin is very similar to a share of a government money market fund. Its assets are public, its promise to pay at par and on demand is private, and no explicit public backstop protects its holders. In the money matrix it is public shadow money (see [Cash Pools and Shadow Money](15-cash-pools-and-shadow-money.md)). It differs from a fund share in two ways.

- **No interest.** The holder earns nothing, so the business of the issuer is to keep the yield of the Treasury bills.
- **Any holder.** Anyone with a mobile phone, anywhere in the world, can hold it.

A stablecoin is thus a new way to carry dollars outside the United States and outside the banks.

## Issuers as Cash Pools

The issuers of stablecoins are themselves very large cash pools. At the end of September 2026, Tether had total assets of $191.45 billion against liabilities of $183.50 billion, so its excess reserves were $7.95 billion.

In May 2026, Tether stated that its exposure of about $141 billion to Treasuries made it the 17th largest holder of US Treasuries in the world, a ranking that compares it with countries. Gold, bitcoin, loans and shares, however, are not public money, and none of them is a permitted reserve under the GENIUS Act. All stablecoin issuers together held $153 billion of Treasury bills in December 2025. An inflow of $3.5 billion into stablecoins lowers the yield of three-month bills by 0.71 basis points on impact and by up to 4 basis points within 10 days.

## Bank Deposits in the Reserves

The GENIUS Act seeks stability, but it allows bank deposits among the reserves. For a cash pool, a large deposit is an uninsured, unsecured claim on one bank. In March 2023, Circle held $3.3 billion of the reserves of USDC, about 8% of the total, at Silicon Valley Bank. When the bank failed, USDC fell to 86 cents at its lowest. It returned to par only after the Treasury, the Fed and the FDIC announced on 12 March that all depositors of the bank would be protected, and fully on 13 March, when redemptions began again. A rule meant to make stablecoins safe can thus put them at risk.

Europe goes further. Under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), an issuer of e-money tokens, the stablecoins that reference one official currency, must always keep at least 30% of the funds that it receives in separate accounts at credit institutions. For a significant token, the minimum is 60% of each currency that it references. The law thus requires issuers to hold a large share of an asset that is not very safe for them.

## Tokenized Deposits

A tokenized deposit is much easier to classify. It is an ordinary bank deposit recorded on a blockchain or with other distributed ledger technology. It stays a liability of the bank, backed by the loans of the bank, with the discount window behind the bank and the same deposit insurance up to the legal limit. It therefore has the same place in the hierarchy of money as any other deposit. The technology of the ledger changes, but the balance sheet behind it does not, and the balance sheet is what matters.

## Payment Accounts at the Fed

In October 2025, Fed Governor Christopher Waller described a "payment account", which he also called a "skinny" master account. On 20 May 2026 the Fed proposed it. Legally eligible institutions, including uninsured ones, could use it only to clear and settle payments directly in central bank money, on Fedwire Funds, FedNow and the National Settlement Service, and for free-of-payment transfers of securities.

- The balance earns no interest.
- The account gets no intraday credit, so a payment that would overdraw it is rejected.
- The holder has no access to the discount window.
- The Reserve Bank sets a limit on the end-of-day balance, based on the expected payments of the holder and not above $1 billion.

The proposal does not widen the legal eligibility for an account. Comments closed on 27 July 2026, and by late November 2026 the Fed had not taken a final decision. If it goes ahead, a stablecoin issuer with a banking licence could hold part of its reserves at the Fed, at the top of the hierarchy of money. An account at the Fed, however, does not make the Fed its lender of last resort. The monetary authorities clearly want to keep the new developments in the money market close to them and under control.
