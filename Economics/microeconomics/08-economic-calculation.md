# Economic Calculation

Each human action implies a plan. The actor chooses an end, selects the means, and acts. But the future is not certain. Nobody can be sure that his action will succeed, which means that he will reach his end, even if he acts rationally. Mises writes that "the uncertainty of the future is already implied in the very notion of action." If a person knew the future with certainty, he would not need to choose or to act. Thus, the actor is always uncertain about the benefits and the subjective costs of his actions.

Jesús Huerta de Soto expresses this idea with a wordplay in Spanish: "El futuro es un por hacer, no un porvenir." The future is something that we must make, not something that simply comes to us. Each action changes the future, so nobody can know it in advance.

## Costs and Benefits

Each action has a cost and a benefit for the actor.

- **Cost.** The cost is the [opportunity cost](02-opportunity-cost.md) of the action. It is the value of the most valued end that the actor gives up when he chooses this action.
- **Benefit.** The benefit is the difference between the value of the end that the actor reaches and the value of the cost. Mises calls it gain or profit, and he explains that this profit "in this primary sense is purely subjective". It is a psychic phenomenon, an increase in the satisfaction of the actor, and nobody can measure it.

Economic calculation is the comparison of the subjective value of the end with the subjective value of the cost. All humans do economic calculation continuously, often without being aware of it. A person who decides to walk to work instead of taking a taxi compares the value of the money that he saves with the value of the time and effort that he spends.

## Ex Ante and Ex Post

Economic calculation can happen at two moments.

1. **Ex ante (before the action).** The actor estimates the benefit that he expects from the action. He cannot know the outcome, because it did not happen yet. He acts only if he expects a profit, that is, if he values the expected end more than its cost. If he expects a loss, he does not act.
2. **Ex post (after the action).** The actor evaluates the result. He compares the end that he reached with the alternative ends that he could have reached with a different action, or with no action. If he values the end more than the alternatives, he made a profit. If he values the alternatives more, he made a loss, and he regrets the action.

The ex ante and the ex post calculations can be different, because the future is uncertain. For example, a student chooses to study engineering instead of medicine. Ex ante, he expects that engineering will give him more satisfaction. Ex post, after some years of work, he can conclude that he chose correctly, or that he would prefer to be a doctor. An entrepreneurial error is the difference between the ex ante expectation and the ex post result. The actor learns from this error, and he uses this knowledge in his next calculations.

## Monetary Calculation

Subjective values cannot be measured, so an individual can only rank them. This is enough for the simple decisions of one person. But a complex economy needs a common unit to compare costs and benefits. Mises explains that money prices give this unit. With money prices, an entrepreneur can calculate ex ante whether a project will give a profit, and he can see ex post in his accounts whether it gave a profit or a loss.

For this reason, Mises often uses the term "economic calculation" in a narrower sense, as calculation in money prices. Monetary calculation does not measure subjective value. It is only a tool that helps people estimate their costs and benefits. But without it, nobody could decide which of the many possible uses of scarce resources is the most valued. Mises used this argument in 1920 to show that a socialist economy, without private property and market prices, cannot calculate.
